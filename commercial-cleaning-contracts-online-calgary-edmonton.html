Commercial Cleaning

How to Win Commercial Cleaning Contracts Online in Calgary and Edmonton

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · October 25, 2026 · 7 min read

Commercial buyers are not browsing for fun. Facility managers, office managers and property managers want a reliable supplier they can trust for months or years. Your online presence should answer their questions fast.

Understand who actually signs the contract

Commercial cleaning is rarely one decision maker. An office manager may start the search, a facility manager checks the details, and an owner or board approves the spend. In a condo or strata setting, a property manager brings your name to a board that you may never meet. Your website has to work for all of them at once. The office manager wants a fast answer and a clear next step. The facility manager wants scope, schedule and supervision. The owner wants to feel safe and know the price is fair. Write each commercial page so a tired person skimming on a phone can find those three things in under a minute. Plain language beats corporate jargon every time. Write a commercial page for each building type

A single page called Commercial Cleaning is not enough. A dental clinic, a warehouse office and a retail strip mall have very different needs. Give each major type its own page with its own wording. For a clinic, talk about infection control habits, color coded cloths and after hours access. For a school or daycare, talk about safe products, background checks and summer deep cleans. For an office, talk about nightly service, recycling, kitchens and washrooms. For a condo, talk about common areas, parkades and lobbies. Each page should name the city, such as janitorial services Edmonton or office cleaning Calgary, once or twice in a natural way. Build a simple walk-through request flow

The best commercial leads usually start with a site walk-through. Make asking for one painless. Keep the form short: name, company, building address, building type, approximate size, how often they want service and the best time to call. Add a phone number that you actually answer, and a line that says when you reply, such as the same business day. After they submit, send an automatic email that confirms the request and tells them what happens next. Then call fast. Many property managers contact two or three companies and hire the one that answers first and sounds organized. If you also offer booking for walk-throughs, our booking systems for cleaning companies can let buyers pick a time slot without any back and forth.

Prove you are safe to hire

Commercial buyers carry risk. If your crew damages something or a key goes missing, the manager answers for it. So reduce the risk on the page. Mention that you carry liability insurance and, where it applies, workers compensation coverage, and offer a certificate on request. Explain how you screen and train staff, how you handle keys, alarm codes and access cards, and how you supervise quality. Describe your supplies policy, including safe products and any safety data sheets you can provide. In Alberta, many buyers also ask about WCB clearance, so be ready to share it. In Illinois, they may ask about insurance and bonding. Never claim a certification you do not hold. A short, honest trust section beats a wall of badges, and it is easy to keep accurate.

Win searches in Calgary and Edmonton by area

Commercial buyers often search near their building. In Calgary, think about the quadrants: NW, NE, SW and SE, plus nearby Airdrie, Cochrane and Okotoks. In Edmonton, think about the south side, the west end, Sherwood Park and St. Albert. You do not need a page for every neighborhood. Choose the areas where you can truly deliver service, then create a clear service area page and mention those areas in your commercial pages and Google Business Profile. Do not copy and paste the same text with the city name swapped, because it reads badly and does little good. Add a real detail instead, such as the kinds of buildings you serve there. Use your Google Business Profile for B2B trust

Even commercial clients check your map listing. Keep your categories accurate, such as janitorial service and commercial cleaning service, and write a description that mentions offices, clinics and strata buildings if you serve them. Add photos of your crew in uniform, your equipment and finished work, with permission and without showing private information. Ask long term commercial clients for reviews that mention the type of building and the reliability of the service. A review that says the crew is on time every night is worth more to a facility manager than a generic five stars. Quote clearly and send a proposal that is easy to approve

Many cleaners lose contracts after the walk-through, not before. The fix is a proposal that is simple to read and easy to pass to a boss. Start with a one paragraph summary of what you will do. Then list the scope by area, the service days and times, the supplies you provide, and how problems are reported and fixed. State the price clearly, what is included, and what would count as an extra, such as carpet extraction or window cleaning. Add the term, the notice period and how you handle holidays. Keep it to two or three pages. Offer a short trial period if it suits your business. The price depends on building size, frequency, floor types, washroom count and access, so explain what drives your number rather than just stating it.

Follow up without being pushy

Silence after a proposal does not mean no. Buyers are busy. A good rhythm is a thank you the same day, a short check in after two or three business days, and one more follow up a week later with a helpful point, such as an offer to adjust the scope. Keep a simple spreadsheet or CRM with the company, contact, date, stage and next step. After you win or lose, note why. Over a few months, patterns show up. Maybe you lose on price in one segment, or win every clinic. Use that to sharpen your pages and your outreach. Direct outreach also helps: introduce yourself to local property management firms and offer a free walk-through. Measure what matters for commercial leads

Track more than clicks. Count form submissions and phone calls from commercial pages separately from residential ones. Note which source each lead came from: Google search, map listing, ad, referral or outreach. Ask on the first call, how did you hear about us. Track the number of walk-throughs booked, proposals sent and contracts won. Because one commercial contract can last for years, a small number of wins matters a lot. Review the numbers monthly, not daily, and change one thing at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find commercial cleaning clients?

Combine local search visibility, a commercial-focused website, referrals and direct outreach.

Do property managers search on Google?

Yes. Many start with Google and check reviews and websites before requesting quotes.

Do you help janitorial companies outside Alberta?

Yes. We also work with commercial cleaners across Canada and in the US.

How long does it take to win a commercial cleaning contract?

It depends on the buyer. Some small offices decide within days of a walk-through. Larger buildings, schools and strata boards can take weeks or months because of approvals and tenders. Plan for a longer sales cycle than residential work, and keep following up politely.

Should I list prices on my commercial cleaning pages?

Usually not exact prices, because building size, frequency and access change the cost a lot. Instead, explain what affects the price and invite a walk-through. This sets expectations, filters out poor fits and keeps you from underquoting before you have seen the space.

Do I need a separate website for commercial and residential cleaning?

Not usually. One site with clearly separated residential and commercial sections works well and shares authority. A separate site can make sense if you run two distinct brands. We can review your setup on a call and advise what suits your situation.

What should a commercial cleaning proposal include?

Include a scope by area, service days and times, supplies, supervision and quality checks, insurance details, price and extras, contract term and notice period. Keep it short and clear so a manager can approve it without needing to ask many questions.
