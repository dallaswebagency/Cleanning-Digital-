AI Search

How to Get Your Cleaning Company Recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · November 21, 2026 · 23 min read

A homeowner in Tampa types a question into ChatGPT: "Who is a reliable move-out cleaning company near me that does the oven and the fridge?" A property manager in Chicago asks Google's AI Overview for "janitorial companies for a 20,000 square foot office, insured, with night crews." A busy parent in Austin asks Perplexity for "a house cleaner who does recurring weekly visits and uses pet-safe products."

Each of those people gets a short answer with a handful of company names. They do not scroll through ten blue links. They do not compare forty websites. They call one of the names they were given.

If your company is one of those names, you just got a lead that cost you nothing. If it is not, you never even knew the conversation happened.

This guide is the practical, step by step version of getting into those answers. It is written for cleaning company owners in the United States, with examples drawn from the four states we focus on: Florida, Michigan, Illinois and Texas. If you want the background on how these tools work, read our explainer on how ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews choose cleaning companies first. This article is the playbook that comes after it.

The short version: AI tools recommend businesses they can find, verify and describe in one confident sentence. You get there by fixing four things: a complete and consistent business footprint, a website with clear pages and direct answers, a steady flow of recent reviews, and mentions on sources the AI tools already trust. Nobody can guarantee a placement, but each of these steps removes a reason for an AI tool to skip you.

What "AI search" actually means for a cleaning company

"AI search" is not one thing. When owners say it, they usually mean four different products, and each one behaves a little differently.

  • Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. These appear inside Google Search. They draw on Google's own index and, for local questions, on the same signals that power the map results, such as your Google Business Profile, reviews and website.
  • ChatGPT. When it searches the web, it pulls from a mix of web pages and, for local queries, from business data and review sources. Without web search turned on, it answers from what it learned in training, which can be out of date.
  • Gemini. Google's assistant. It leans on Google's index and Google business data, so a strong Google Business Profile helps here more than anywhere else.
  • Perplexity. It shows its sources next to every answer, which makes it the easiest to study. If you want to learn how AI tools find companies like yours, Perplexity is the best classroom because it tells you which pages it used.

The details of each tool change often, and the companies behind them do not publish exact ranking rules. So we will not pretend there is a secret formula. What we can say is that these tools have a shared appetite. They want facts they can verify from more than one place, and they want to describe a business accurately without guessing.

Google has said publicly that you do not need special markup or a special file to appear in its AI features, and that the same fundamentals of good search still apply. We take that at face value. Everything in this guide is built on fundamentals, not tricks.

Why this matters more for cleaning companies than for most trades

Cleaning is a trust purchase. Someone is letting your team into their home or their building. When a person asks an AI tool for a recommendation, they are outsourcing the first round of trust checking, and the tool will tend to favor companies that look safe: lots of recent reviews, a real address or a clear service area, insurance mentioned, a professional website, and consistent details everywhere.

That works in favor of the small owner who does the basics well. A company with 90 recent, detailed reviews and a clear website can be recommended ahead of a bigger competitor with a thin online presence. AI tools do not care how many trucks you own. They care whether they can find consistent proof that you exist and do good work.

It also creates a quiet risk. If your details are inconsistent or thin, an AI tool may simply leave you out, or worse, repeat an old phone number, a wrong service area, or a competitor's attributes. We have seen business listings that still show a company that rebranded two years ago. An AI tool cannot tell which version is right unless the web agrees with itself.

Step 1: Run your baseline test before you change anything

You cannot improve what you have not measured. Spend an hour on this before you touch your website.

Open a notes document or a spreadsheet. Then run the following prompts in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google (look for the AI Overview). Use a private or logged out window where you can, because results can shift with your history and location.

The prompt set

Write 12 to 15 prompts that real customers would type. Mix these types:

  • Direct local: "Best house cleaning service in Naperville, Illinois"
  • Service specific: "Move out cleaning company in Orlando that does carpets and appliances"
  • Situation based: "I need a deep clean before my in-laws visit next weekend in Grand Rapids"
  • Commercial: "Janitorial company for a medical office in Houston with evening service"
  • Trust based: "Is [your company name] a good cleaning company?"
  • Comparison: "[Your company] vs [competitor] cleaning in Austin"
  • Pricing: "How much does house cleaning cost in Detroit?"

What to record

For each prompt and each tool, note whether you appear, where you appear in the list, which competitors appear, what the tool says about you, and which sources it cites. Take screenshots. The sources are the most valuable part, because they are the pages you need to be present on.

Do this once now and again every month. Keep the same prompts so you can compare. Treat the result as a snapshot, not a score. AI answers vary from one run to the next, so the pattern across 15 prompts matters more than any single answer.

Step 2: Fix your business footprint

Your footprint is the set of places on the internet that describe your business. AI tools cross check these. When they agree, you look real. When they disagree, you look risky.

Your Google Business Profile comes first

For Google's own AI features and for Gemini, your profile is the closest thing to a source of truth. Make it complete. That means the right primary category, secondary categories that match real services, accurate hours, a clear service area, a description written in plain English, real photos of your team and work, and every service listed with a short description.

We cover the full setup in our guide to Google Business Profile for cleaning companies. If your profile has been sitting untouched for a year, fix it before anything else in this article. It is the cheapest and fastest win on the page.

Name, address and phone must match everywhere

Pick one exact version of your business name, phone number, website address and service area description. Then compare it across:

  • Your Google Business Profile
  • Your website header, footer and contact page
  • Apple Business Connect and Bing Places
  • Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor and LinkedIn
  • Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor and any cleaning marketplaces you are on
  • Your Better Business Bureau listing, if you have one
  • Chamber of commerce and local association listings

A mismatch like "Sparkle Clean LLC" on one site and "Sparkle Clean Services" on another is the type of small difference that makes a tool unsure whether they are the same company. Fix the old ones, claim duplicate listings and merge or remove them.

Service area honesty

If you are a service area business without a storefront, say so clearly and list the cities you actually cover. Overstating your reach is a common mistake, and it backfires when an AI tool repeats a city you do not really serve and sends the wrong customers to you. Be specific. "We serve Naperville, Aurora, Plainfield and Oswego" is better than "all of the Chicago suburbs."

If you serve several cities, give each major one its own page on your site. That is what our location pages do for the markets we cover, and it is the pattern we recommend in local SEO for cleaning companies.

Step 3: Make your website easy for an AI tool to quote

AI tools do not read your site the way a person does. They break it into chunks and look for chunks that directly answer a question. Your job is to make those chunks easy to find and easy to trust.

Write pages that answer questions in the first two sentences

Take any service page. Look at the first paragraph under each heading. Does it answer the heading's question straight away?

A weak version: "At Sparkle Clean we believe in a fresh start for every client, and our passion for cleanliness is unmatched."

A strong version: "A standard move-out cleaning in Orlando usually takes two to four people three to six hours, depending on the size of the home. It includes inside the oven, fridge, cabinets and all bathrooms, and it is priced by square footage and condition."

The second version can be lifted straight into an AI answer. The first cannot. This is the single most useful rewrite you can do, and it costs nothing but time. Go through your top service pages and rewrite the opening of each section so that it states a fact, a range, a process or a rule.

Have one page for each service and each major city

One long "Services" page with every offering in a bullet list is hard for any search system to use. A page for "Move-out cleaning in Orlando" and another for "Deep cleaning in Orlando" gives the tool a clear match for a specific question. The principle is simple: each page should have its own substance, not the same text with the city name swapped.

Show your proof on the page

AI tools and customers both want to see that you are real. Put these on your site:

  • Your real address or service area, and your phone number
  • Insurance and bonding statements, stated plainly
  • Background check and training policies
  • Your team, with real names and real photos
  • Reviews you have collected, with the source named
  • Before and after photos from actual jobs, with the neighborhood noted
  • Your guarantee or re-clean policy in writing

A website built by a team that knows cleaning companies will have these in the right places. If yours is a template with placeholder text, that is worth fixing, and our web design for cleaning companies service starts exactly there.

Add a genuine FAQ section to every important page

Real questions, real answers, written in plain language. "How long does a deep clean take?" "Do I need to be home?" "Do you bring your own supplies?" "What is your cancellation policy?" "Do you clean after construction?"

A good FAQ does double duty. It helps the customer who is about to book, and it provides tidy question and answer pairs that AI tools can use. Mark them up with FAQ schema so the structure is explicit. Google has narrowed how it displays FAQ results in regular search, but the questions and answers still serve readers and still help systems understand your page.

Use structured data where it is accurate

Schema is the label on the box. It does not create value that is not there, but it removes ambiguity. For a cleaning company, the useful types are LocalBusiness (or a more specific type like HouseCleaning where appropriate), Service, FAQPage, Review summaries where you have the right to show them, and BreadcrumbList. Keep the details in your schema identical to your visible content and your Google profile. Never mark up reviews you do not have or claims you cannot support.

Step 4: Build the review engine AI tools lean on

Reviews are the closest thing a local business has to a public reputation score, and AI tools notice them. Not only the star rating, but the language inside them.

Volume and recency

A company with 400 reviews from three years ago looks less alive than a company with 120 reviews and ten new ones every month. Aim for a steady flow, not a burst. A simple routine works: ask every satisfied customer at the end of the first clean, send a text with a direct link, and follow up once.

Our guide on getting more Google reviews gives you the timing and wording. Do not offer money or gifts in exchange for reviews, and do not review gate (asking only happy customers). Platforms prohibit both, and a penalty would cost you far more than the reviews are worth.

Detail beats stars

When customers mention the service, the area and the result, those details become searchable context. A review that says "They did a deep clean of our kitchen and two bathrooms in Tampa Palms and the grout looks new" is more useful than "Great service!" You cannot write reviews for customers, but you can prompt them: "If you have a moment, it helps others to hear what service we did and what you noticed most."

Reply to every review

Replies are a free chance to restate your services and service areas in natural language. Thank the person, mention the service, and keep it human. A reply to a negative review that is calm and specific is read by prospects and, in practice, by the tools that summarize your reputation.

Step 5: Get mentioned where AI tools already look

When you run your baseline test in Perplexity or Google's AI features, you will notice the same sources appear again and again. For local services, they usually include:

  • Google Business Profile and Google Maps data
  • Yelp
  • Facebook and Nextdoor
  • Better Business Bureau
  • Angi, Thumbtack and similar marketplaces
  • Local news sites and "best of" lists
  • Chamber of commerce and trade association directories
  • Reddit threads where people ask for recommendations
  • Industry lists such as ISSA, ARCSI or local property management association pages

You will not be on all of them, and you do not need to be. Look at the sources your own test surfaced for your city, and aim to be present and accurate on the top five.

Earn real local mentions

Local press and community pages carry weight because they are independent. Some ideas that work for cleaning companies:

  • Sponsor a youth team, a charity run or a school fundraiser, and ask for a link from the organization's site.
  • Offer a free cleaning to a local nonprofit or a family in need and let a local news outlet or community group tell the story.
  • Join your chamber of commerce and fill in the member profile completely.
  • Partner with real estate agents, property managers and home stagers who can mention you on their sites.
  • Contribute a short, practical tip to a local news outlet when a seasonal topic comes up, such as spring cleaning, post-storm cleanup or allergy season.

None of these is fast. All of them build the kind of independent proof that makes you easier to recommend.

Be careful with "AI visibility" shortcuts

You will meet offers for paid listings, fake review packages and "guaranteed AI ranking" services. Avoid them. Nobody outside these companies controls their outputs, and a guaranteed placement is a red flag. Fake reviews risk removal and public damage. Spending the same money on a better website and a review routine is a far better bet.

Step 6: Publish content that earns citations

Blog posts are not magic, but a smaller set of genuinely useful pages can make you the source an AI tool prefers to quote.

Write what customers actually ask

Mine your own inbox, your phone calls and your booking forms. What do people ask before they hire you? Turn the top 20 into articles or FAQ entries. Examples that work well for cleaning companies:

  • How much does house cleaning cost in [your city]? (give honest ranges and what changes them)
  • What is the difference between a deep clean and a standard clean?
  • What should I do before the cleaners arrive?
  • How often should I have my office cleaned?
  • What does a move-out cleaning checklist include?
  • Should I tip my cleaner, and how much?

When you publish pricing ranges, be honest and label them as typical ranges. AI tools favor clear, specific answers over vague ones, and customers do too. If price transparency worries you, remember that a range with a clear explanation of what moves the price filters out the wrong leads and attracts the right ones.

Add local depth competitors cannot copy

A generic article about deep cleaning will be outranked by the big national sites. An article about "Spring cleaning in Michigan: dealing with road salt residue on floors and entryways" or "Hurricane season cleanup for Florida homeowners" has a smaller audience but far less competition, and it shows real local knowledge. For our Michigan and Florida markets we write exactly this sort of local context into the location pages.

Keep the content current

Add a visible updated date to articles and refresh them at least once a year. Out of date prices and old statistics are exactly the kind of thing that makes an AI tool trust a competitor's page over yours.

Step 7: Match the way each tool tends to work

The tools differ, and a little tuning helps.

For Google AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini

Focus on your Google Business Profile, your website's technical health and your reviews on Google. Make sure your pages load fast on mobile, are indexable, and have clear headings. Check Google Search Console for indexing errors and use the URL inspection tool when you publish something new. If your site is built on a drag and drop builder that blocks crawling or loads slowly, that is worth fixing, as we explain in why Wix, GoDaddy and Squarespace sites stop ranking.

For ChatGPT

ChatGPT's browsing tends to draw on web pages and widely used business data sources. Make sure you are accurate on Yelp, Bing Places, Facebook and your own site. Allowing common AI crawlers in your robots.txt file, rather than blocking them by accident, is a sensible step if you want to be discoverable. Check what your site's robots.txt says today, because some website security plugins block these crawlers by default.

For Perplexity

Perplexity shows its sources. Study which pages it cites for your prompts and make sure you appear on those or have a strong page that answers the same question better. Clear, quotable paragraphs matter most here.

For all of them

Be consistent, be specific and be easy to verify. If a tool has to guess, it will often choose someone else.

Step 8: Track what is working

Tracking AI visibility is still imperfect, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. You can still build a simple system.

Your monthly scorecard

  • Prompt test results: how many of your 15 prompts mention you, in which tools, and in what position
  • Branded searches: use Google Search Console to watch how many people search for your company name directly
  • Direct and referral traffic: a rise can come from people who heard your name in an AI answer and typed your address
  • Calls and form fills: ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" and add "AI assistant (ChatGPT, Google, etc.)" as an answer option on your booking form
  • Review count and recency: reviews gained this month, average rating, and reply rate
  • Google Business Profile insights: calls, direction requests and website clicks

Be honest about attribution

Some AI-driven leads will show up as "direct" traffic or as people who call without clicking. That is normal. Use the question on your form and a quick call-intake script to catch more of them. Within three months you will have a rough but useful picture.

A 90 day plan you can start this week

Days 1 to 14: Baseline and cleanup

  • Run the prompt test across all four tools and save the results
  • Fix your Google Business Profile completely
  • Audit name, address and phone details on at least 15 directories
  • Claim or correct Apple, Bing, Yelp and Facebook listings
  • Check your robots.txt and make sure you are not blocking search or AI crawlers

Days 15 to 45: Website and proof

  • Rewrite the first paragraph of every service page to answer the question directly
  • Add or improve a page for each core service and each priority city
  • Add a real FAQ to every important page, with FAQ schema
  • Add proof: insurance, team photos, background check policy, guarantee and real reviews
  • Launch the review routine and aim for at least 8 to 10 new reviews

Days 46 to 90: Authority and content

  • Publish four to six helpful articles from your real customer questions
  • Earn at least three independent local mentions or links (sponsorship, chamber, partner site)
  • Reply to every review in natural language
  • Re-run the prompt test and compare to your baseline
  • Pick the three weakest prompts and write a page or section specifically for each

By the end of day 90, you will have a stronger site, a stronger Google presence and a clear record of progress. Whether or not a given AI tool recommends you, the work pays off on regular Google searches, because it is the same work.

Common mistakes we see

  • Chasing AI tricks before fixing the basics. An incomplete Google profile and a thin website will hurt you far more than a missing llms.txt file.
  • Keyword stuffing for AI. Writing unnatural text to impress a machine makes pages worse for people and for the machines.
  • Ignoring reviews. A company with great work and few recent reviews gets passed over.
  • Letting directories go stale. Old phone numbers and closed addresses spread and get repeated.
  • Publishing generic AI-written articles. If your content could appear on any cleaning site in any city, it adds nothing. Real local detail, real numbers and real experience are what get quoted.
  • Overstating your service area. Listing cities you do not serve confuses tools and wastes your time on bad leads.
  • Buying "guaranteed AI rankings." Nobody can guarantee them.
  • Measuring once and giving up. AI answers shift. Check monthly and look at the trend.

How AI search changes the way you should think about your website

For years, a cleaning company website was a brochure. You put up services, a phone number and a contact form. In an AI-first world, your website is also a source of facts that machines read and repeat. That raises the bar in three ways.

First, clarity beats cleverness. A tagline like "Where clean meets care" tells nobody anything. "Residential and move-out cleaning in Orlando, insured and background-checked, with a 24 hour re-clean guarantee" tells a person and a machine exactly who you are.

Second, completeness beats minimalism. A three-page website with a gallery and a form leaves AI tools with little to quote. A site with a clear page for each service and city, an honest FAQ and a visible proof section gives them plenty.

Third, consistency beats volume. Ten consistent profiles are worth more than fifty contradictory ones. Quality control of your footprint is quietly one of the most valuable tasks in local marketing.

What to do if an AI tool says something wrong about you

It will happen eventually. A tool lists the wrong phone number, says you serve a city you do not, or repeats a bad review from years ago. Here is what to do.

  • Find the source. Ask the tool to show where it got the claim, or check which sites it cites. The error almost always lives on a directory, an old page or a third-party listing.
  • Fix it at the source. Update or remove the wrong listing. Contact the site owner if it is not yours.
  • Strengthen the correct version. Make sure your own site and your Google profile state the correct fact clearly, in plain text.
  • Report it where the tool allows. Most AI products have a feedback button on each answer. Use it for clear factual errors.
  • Recheck in a few weeks. Tools do not update instantly, and an answer may take time to change.

Where AI search is heading, and what to do about it

Nobody knows exactly how these products will evolve, but a few directions look likely. AI tools will keep getting better at local questions. They will lean harder on verified business data and reviews. More customers will use them as a first stop, especially for considered purchases like recurring cleaning and commercial contracts. And Google's regular results and its AI features will keep blending.

You do not need to predict the details. The practical response is the same as for the last fifteen years of local search: be the most findable, most verifiable, most helpful business in your area, and keep proving it with fresh evidence. AI search simply raises the reward for doing that well and the cost of doing it badly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my competitor rank higher than me on Google?

Usually they have a stronger Google Business Profile, a separate page for each service and area, more recent reviews, and more local websites linking to them. Their website being nicer is rarely the reason.

Can I pay Google to rank higher in the map results?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can run ads, but they appear separately and are marked as ads.

How do I get ChatGPT to recommend my cleaning company?

There is no guaranteed method. The basics that help on Google tend to help here too: a complete Google profile, steady reviews, consistent details across the web and a helpful website with a page for each service and area.

Do I need special code or an llms.txt file for AI search?

Google says you don't need special markup or an llms.txt file to appear in its AI features. Focus on helpful pages and strong basics first.

How long will it take to overtake a competitor?

It depends on how far ahead they are and what is holding you back. Anyone who promises a date is guessing. A realistic goal is to fix the basics in 30 days, then keep improving each month.

Does distance matter, and can I change it?

Yes, Google lists distance as one of three factors. You can't move closer to customers, but you can improve relevance and prominence, which is where most of the gains are.
