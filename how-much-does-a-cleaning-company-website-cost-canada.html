How Much Does a Cleaning Company Website Cost in Canada? What Affects the Price

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · October 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Prices vary widely, from low-cost templates to full custom builds. Instead of a single number, here are the factors that actually decide the price, so you can compare quotes fairly.

Number and type of pages

A one-page site costs less than a site with service pages, area pages and a blog. See what we recommend in pages every cleaning website needs.

Custom design or template

What actually drives the price of a cleaning website

Cost is not random. It depends on a handful of things you can see in advance. The number of pages matters, because every service and area page needs writing and design. Custom design takes more time than a template. Features such as online booking, quote calculators, client portals and payment add build and testing work. Content is a big one: do you have real photos and clear copy, or do we need to create them? Then there is search setup, speed work and tracking. Finally, who is building it matters, from a solo freelancer to a specialist team. We do not quote fixed numbers in an article because every business differs. Our pricing page shows how packages are structured, and a short conversation gives you a real figure.

Template, custom or something in between

A template site starts from a ready layout and swaps in your logo, colours and text. It can be fast and fair for a new company, but it often looks like everyone else and can be hard to tune for conversions. A fully custom site is planned around your services and customers, which usually means clearer pages and a stronger first impression, with more time involved. A middle path uses a proven structure with custom sections, copy and photos. Ask any provider which of these you are getting and what is truly custom. Our cleaning company website design work sits on the custom side but reuses tested patterns, so you do not pay to reinvent the basics.

Build cost versus the ongoing costs people forget

The build is one cost. Hosting, domain renewal, security, backups, plugin or software licences and updates are the ongoing ones. Add content changes, new service pages and seasonal offers over time. If you plan to run ads or invest in SEO, those are separate budgets again. A cheap build with no maintenance can become expensive when something breaks or gets hacked. Ask what is included after launch, how fast fixes happen and who owns the site and domain. Our website maintenance for cleaning companies covers the routine care so you can focus on running crews. Write the ongoing items on a simple list before you sign anything, and compare providers on the whole picture.

Questions to ask before you sign with a web designer

Ask to see work for cleaning companies or other local service businesses, and look at them on your phone. Ask how the site will be set up to get calls and quote requests, not just how it will look. Ask who writes the copy, who sources the photos and what happens if you need changes. Ask what is included in launch: speed, mobile design, basic search setup, tracking and schema. Ask about ownership of the domain, hosting and files. Ask how long the project takes and what you need to supply. You can browse examples in our project gallery and compare how different sites are structured.

Warning signs of a quote that is too good to be true

Be careful with a very low quote that does not list what is included. Watch for vague promises such as SEO included with no detail, lock-in contracts, hidden hosting fees or a refusal to give you access to your own site. Be wary of copied content and stock photos used everywhere, since search engines and customers can both tell. Watch out for providers who cannot explain how the site will generate enquiries. A proper quote lists pages, features, revisions, timelines and what happens after launch. If something is unclear, ask in writing. A good partner is happy to explain. If you would like a clear written scope, book a discovery call and we will walk through it.

Ways to make a website budget go further

Start with the pages that earn money: home, your top services, areas, reviews, contact and quote. Add secondary pages after launch. Supply real photos of your team and results, since they beat stock images and reduce design time. Write down your common questions so FAQ content is quick to produce. Pick features by need: if you take bookings by phone and have little volume, you may not need a full system yet, though a booking and quote system pays off as you grow. Plan the site so pages can be added later without a rebuild. Read our list of must have pages for cleaning websites in Canada to decide what comes first.

Think in terms of return, not just cost

A website is a sales tool, so judge it by what it produces. Work out what a typical new customer is worth over a year, and what share of enquiries you close. If one extra recurring client pays for a meaningful part of the site, the picture changes. A cheaper site that gets no enquiries costs more than a better one that does. Track calls, form fills and booked jobs from day one, and review them monthly. Pair the site with a tuned Google profile, as shown in our Google Business Profile tips, so visitors actually arrive. We cannot promise specific results, because they depend on your market, reviews and follow-up speed.

Local factors for Calgary, other Canadian cities and Illinois

Local relevance affects both design and cost. A Calgary cleaner may want pages for NW, NE, SW and SE, plus Airdrie, Cochrane and Okotoks, while an Edmonton or Toronto business needs its own areas. Bilingual content for Quebec or parts of Ontario adds work. Canadian businesses often need clear wording about GST, insurance and privacy. If you also serve the US, such as cleaning company website design in North Aurora, IL, Aurora and Naperville, plan separate area pages and local details. More areas means more pages, which means more cost, but also more chances to be found. Our local SEO service explains how to add them without thin or copied text.

Plan your timeline and what you need to supply

A smooth project depends on you as much as the builder. Before kickoff, gather your logo, brand colours, service list, service areas, insurance details, reviews and as many real photos as you can. Decide who approves content, because slow feedback adds weeks. Agree on milestones: structure, design, content, testing and launch. Rushed projects cost more and often miss details. If you are comparing options, our pricing overview explains what each stage involves and where time is spent.

Add features in phases instead of all at once

You do not need every feature on day one. Launch with the core pages, a quote form and tracking. After a month or two, add what customers ask for: online booking, a service area map, a before and after gallery or a commercial quote request page. Phasing spreads cost and lets real data guide choices. It also keeps risk low, since you test each addition before building the next. A booking system is a common second phase for busy residential cleaners, while commercial firms often add a detailed capabilities page first.

Hiring a freelancer versus an agency

A freelancer can be a good fit for a small, simple site, and may cost less, but you rely on one person who may be unavailable later. An agency brings design, development, content and search skills, and continuity, though it may be pricier and have minimum scopes. Ask who will actually do the work, how support works and what happens if the contact leaves. Check references from service businesses. Whichever route you choose, insist on clear ownership of your domain and files. If you want an agency view, book a discovery call and compare it to your other quotes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do cleaning website prices vary so much?

Scope, design depth, SEO work and support differ greatly between providers.

Is it cheaper to use a website builder?

It can be cheaper upfront, but custom work often performs better for lead generation.

Do you work with Canadian and US cleaning companies?

Yes. Calgary is our current focus, and we also work with companies across Canada and in places like North Aurora, Illinois.

Can I get a cleaning website for a small budget?

Yes, if you keep the scope tight: a few core pages, a clear call to action, real photos and a mobile friendly layout. You can add pages and features as the business grows. What you get depends on what you include.

Do I pay for the website once or monthly?

It varies by provider. Many projects have a build cost plus ongoing hosting and maintenance. Some offer monthly plans. Ask for a written list of what is one time and what recurs, including renewals.

How long does it take to build a cleaning company website?

It depends on page count, features and how quickly you provide photos and approvals. A focused site can take a few weeks. Delays usually come from missing content, so gather logos and photos early.

Will the website bring clients without SEO or ads?

A good site converts visitors, but it needs traffic. That comes from your Google profile, local SEO, referrals, reviews or ads. Plan how people will find the site as part of the project.
