Why Your Cleaning Company Website Is Not Getting Quote Requests

By the CleaningDigital Team · October 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Plenty of cleaning companies have a website and still get most of their work from referrals. If your site rarely produces quote requests, the problem is usually not traffic. It is what happens after someone lands on the page.

The quote path is hidden

Visitors should see how to request a quote within a few seconds. Put a clear button in the header, in the hero and at the end of every service page. A short form with name, phone, service type and a message is enough.

The site is slow on mobile

Most people search for cleaning services on their phones. Large uncompressed photos and heavy plugins make pages slow, and slow pages lose visitors before they read a word. Compress images, limit scripts and test on a real phone.

Services are vague

A page that says “we clean everything” does not rank and does not convince. Create a separate page for each core service, such as deep cleaning, move-out cleaning or office cleaning, and explain what is included.

There is no proof

New customers are letting strangers into their home or workplace. Show real reviews, your service area, insurance details and clear photos of your work. Trust removes the last bit of hesitation.

Nobody can find the site

A great site still needs visibility. Service pages, city pages and a complete Google Business Profile help you appear when people search for cleaning near them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages does a cleaning company website need?

Most cleaning companies do well with a home page, one page per core service, a few city pages, an about page and a contact page. Add more as you grow.

Should my quote form be short or long?

Keep it short. Name, phone, email, service type and a short message are enough. You can ask detailed questions once you speak with the customer.

Does website speed really affect leads?

Yes. Slow pages frustrate visitors, especially on mobile, and they are more likely to leave and call a competitor.

Do I need a new website or can I fix my current one?

It depends on the site. A free audit shows whether targeted fixes will work or whether a redesign is the better investment.
