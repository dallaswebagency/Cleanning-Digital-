Why Your Cleaning Company Website Is Not Getting Quote Requests

By the CleaningDigital Team · October 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Plenty of cleaning companies have a website and still get most of their work from referrals. If your site rarely produces quote requests, the problem is usually not traffic. It is what happens after someone lands on the page. Our cleaning company website design and local SEO services fix these problems.

The quote path is hidden

Visitors should see how to request a quote within a few seconds. Put a clear button in the header, in the hero and at the end of every service page. A short form with name, phone, service type and a message is enough.

The site is slow on mobile

Most people search for cleaning services on their phones. Large uncompressed photos and heavy plugins make pages slow, and slow pages lose visitors before they read a word. Compress images, limit scripts and test on a real phone.

Services are vague

A page that says “we clean everything” does not rank and does not convince. Create a separate page for each core service, such as deep cleaning, move-out cleaning or office cleaning, and explain what is included.

There is no proof

New customers are letting strangers into their home or workplace. Show real reviews, your service area, insurance details and clear photos of your work. Trust removes the last bit of hesitation.

Nobody can find the site

A great site still needs visibility. Service pages, city pages and a complete Google Business Profile help you appear when people search for cleaning near them.

Run a five second test on your home page

Ask a friend who has never seen your site to look at the home page for five seconds on their phone, then close it. Ask three things. What does this company do? Where does it work? How would you ask for a price? If they hesitate on any of them, your visitors do too. Most cleaning company websites fail this test because the hero says something like “Excellence in cleaning since 2012” instead of “House and office cleaning in NW Calgary, free quotes in one business day.” Put the service, the place and the next step in the first screen. Then repeat the test on a service page and on your contact page. This costs nothing and it often shows you the biggest leak in about ten minutes. Read your site like a nervous homeowner

Imagine you are about to give a stranger a key to your house. What would you want to know? Are they insured? Do they send the same person each time? What happens if something breaks? Are products pet safe? Most cleaning websites answer none of this. Add a short section on every service page covering who comes, how they are screened, what is covered by insurance and what to do if you are not happy. Use plain language and short sentences. Add a photo of your real team or vehicle if you have one. Trust is the main thing you are selling, and the page should say so out loud instead of hoping the visitor assumes it. A good cleaning company website design builds these trust blocks into the layout so they never get forgotten.

Fix the form before you fix anything else

Open your quote form on your phone and fill it in as if you were a customer. Count the fields. Check that the keyboard shows numbers for the phone field. Check that the button is large enough for a thumb. Submit it and see how long the confirmation takes. Then check where the email went. Many owners find that forms send to an old address, land in spam or quietly fail. Add a clear thank you message that says when you will reply, such as “We reply within one business day.” Also add a visible phone number as a tap to call button. Some people will never fill in a form, and they should still have an easy route to you. Test the form once a month, because plugin updates break forms more often than anyone expects.

Write service pages that match how people search

Think about the words a real customer types. In Calgary that might be “move out cleaning SW Calgary” or “office cleaning Beltline.” In North Aurora, Illinois it could be “house cleaning North Aurora IL” or “deep clean Naperville.” Each phrase deserves its own page with a clear heading, a short description of what is included, who it suits, how a visit works and a quote button. Do not copy and paste the same text across cities. Mention real neighbourhoods and the kinds of homes or buildings you clean there. Our post on the must have pages for a cleaning company website in Canada lists which pages to build first, and the local SEO service can map keywords to pages for you.

Show your pricing approach without printing prices

Visitors leave when they cannot tell whether you are affordable. You do not need to publish a rate card to ease that worry. Explain what drives your quote: home size, number of bathrooms, condition of the space, frequency of visits, pets and add ons such as inside the oven. Offer a simple range of options, like a one time deep clean or a recurring plan, and describe what each includes. Then say how fast you can send a quote. This helps serious buyers self qualify and it filters out people who only want the cheapest option. It also cuts down the back and forth calls. If the answer to a question is “it depends,” explain what it depends on. That honesty builds more trust than a vague promise of low prices.

Use photos and proof that look real

Stock photos of smiling people holding spray bottles are everywhere, and visitors skim past them. Use real before and after photos, your team in uniform, your vehicle and your equipment. Get permission before showing any customer space. Add recent reviews with the first name and neighbourhood, and show your insurance, bonding and any association memberships. If you are new and have few reviews, show your process instead: a simple checklist of what happens on the first visit. Compress every image so it loads fast, and add short descriptive alt text. Real proof near the quote button does more than a long paragraph about quality. Our reviews page shows how we think about presenting proof, and you can adapt the same idea to your own site.

Track what visitors actually do

You cannot fix what you cannot see. Set up Google Analytics and Search Console, then track form submissions, phone taps and clicks on the quote button as separate events. Look at which pages bring visitors and which pages lose them. If your service pages get traffic but few quotes, the page needs a clearer offer or a better form. If the home page gets traffic but nobody clicks deeper, the navigation or hero is the problem. Review the numbers monthly, not daily, because small samples mislead. Write down one change per month and note what happened. Over a quarter you will learn more about your own customers than any generic advice can teach you. A simple fix list for the next 30 days

Week one: run the five second test, fix the hero, add a quote button to the header and test the form on your phone. Week two: compress images, remove plugins you do not use and check speed on mobile data. Week three: rewrite your top two service pages with specifics, a short FAQ and a trust section. Week four: add three real reviews near the quote buttons, update your Google Business Profile with the same services and ask your last ten happy customers for a review. Do not try to rebuild everything at once. Small fixes in the right order often double the number of enquiries a site produces, although results depend on your market and your traffic. Answer the follow up questions before they are asked

Think about the last five questions customers asked you before booking. Do you bring your own supplies? Can we be home during the clean? How do you handle keys? What if I need to reschedule? Put those answers on your service pages in a short FAQ, in plain words. Every unanswered question is a reason to leave and call someone else, or to never call at all. A good FAQ also helps you appear in search results and AI answers, because it matches how people really ask. Keep each answer short and honest, and link to the matching service page or your contact page. Update the list every few months as new questions come in from calls and emails.

Make the site work for Calgary and Illinois visitors alike

If you serve more than one market, say so clearly. A visitor in Calgary should see Calgary details fast: quadrants, nearby towns like Airdrie and Cochrane, and Canadian contact details. A visitor in North Aurora or Naperville should see Illinois service areas and a local number. Mixed signals confuse people and search engines. Use separate location pages with their own addresses or service area lists, reviews and photos. Check that your header and footer show the right details on each page. Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages does a cleaning company website need?

Most cleaning companies do well with a home page, one page per core service, a few city pages, an about page and a contact page. Add more as you grow.

Should my quote form be short or long?

Keep it short. Name, phone, email, service type and a short message are enough. You can ask detailed questions once you speak with the customer.

Does website speed really affect leads?

Yes. Slow pages frustrate visitors, especially on mobile, and they are more likely to leave and call a competitor.

Do I need a new website or can I fix my current one?

It depends on the site. A free audit shows whether targeted fixes will work or whether a redesign is the better investment.

Why do I get visitors but no quote requests?

Usually the offer is unclear, the form is hard to find or the page does not build trust. Check mobile speed, the first screen of each page and whether the form actually delivers to your inbox. Fixing these three things is the best place to start.

Should I add live chat to my cleaning website?

Only if someone can answer quickly. A chat box nobody watches looks worse than none. A fast quote form and a tap to call button work well for most small cleaning companies. Add chat later if you can staff it.

How often should I update my website?

Review it monthly and make real changes each quarter. Add new photos, fresh reviews, updated service areas and seasonal offers. Search engines and visitors both prefer sites that look current and cared for.

Will a new design alone get me more quotes?

Not by itself. Design helps, but clear copy, fast loading, trust signals, a simple form and local visibility all matter together. A redesign works best when it follows a plan based on what your current visitors actually do.
