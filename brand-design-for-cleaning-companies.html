Why branding matters for cleaning companies

In a crowded market, many cleaners offer the same services at similar prices. Your brand is what makes a homeowner or property manager feel safe enough to ask for a quote.

  • Look as professional as the larger cleaning franchises
  • Make referrals easier, because people remember your name and logo
  • Support higher quotes for commercial and recurring contracts
  • Keep your website, Google profile, ads and trucks visually consistent
  • Give new staff and subcontractors a clear standard to follow

Service guide

What to expect from brand design.

The practical details cleaning company owners usually ask before they decide.

Who it suits

  • Cleaning companies launching a new business or renaming an existing one.
  • Owners whose logo is hard to read on vehicles, uniforms or phone screens.
  • Companies whose website, Google profile and social pages all look different from one another.

What is included

  • Logo with horizontal, stacked and icon-only versions.
  • Colour palette and font choices that work on screen and in print.
  • A short brand guide covering logo use, colours and tone of voice.
  • Profile images and covers for your website, Google Business Profile and social pages.

What is not included

  • Printing, vehicle wraps and uniform production (we supply files your printer can use).
  • Legal trademark registration or name searches.
  • Ongoing social media posting.

What we need from you

  • Your company name and any logo or colours you already like or dislike.
  • Examples of cleaning brands you admire and ones you do not.
  • Your main customers: homes, offices or both.
  • One person who can give consistent feedback.

How success is measured

  • Whether the brand is applied consistently across the website, Google profile and social pages.
  • Whether customers and staff can describe the company's look and name correctly.
  • Whether the logo stays legible on a phone, a van and a uniform.

What affects timelines and results

  • How many rounds of feedback you need.
  • How quickly you can approve directions.
  • Whether you are starting from nothing or refreshing an existing identity.

Practical examples

  • A logo with fine detail looks fine on a laptop but turns to mush on a van door. The icon-only and stacked versions are built for exactly those places.
  • A company with three slightly different blues across its website, invoices and Google profile looks less established than it is. A shared palette fixes this.

How it works with other services

  • Web design. A new brand is applied directly to your website design, so the two should be planned together.
  • Google Business Profile optimization. Your logo, cover and photos carry through to your Google Business Profile.
