How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Cleaning Company in Calgary and North Aurora

By the CleaningDigital Team · October 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Reviews are the first thing many customers check. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews improves both your map rankings and your conversion rate.

Ask at the right moment

Ask right after a job when the home or office looks great. A text with a direct review link works better than an email buried in a newsletter.

Make it one tap

Use your Google review link or QR code. Fewer steps mean more reviews.

Train your team

Teach crews to mention reviews politely. Never offer money or gifts in exchange for reviews, as this breaks the rules.

Reply to every review

Thank positive reviewers and respond calmly to negative ones. Replies show future customers how you handle problems.

Show reviews on your website

Highlight real reviews on your home and service pages. See how we present proof on our reviews page, and how it fits into a strong cleaning company website.

Connect reviews to your SEO

Reviews feed your Google Business Profile and support local SEO. Read the full picture in our Calgary profile tips.

Set up your review link and QR code properly

Before you ask anyone, make sure the basics work. Open your Google Business Profile, find the option to get more reviews and copy your direct link. Test it on a phone while logged out and while logged in, since many customers will be on a phone. Then shorten it with a link you control, or turn it into a QR code. Put the QR code on invoices, printed receipts, door hangers, a card left after the clean and your van or uniform if it suits your brand. Check it every few months, because a broken link quietly kills your review flow. If you serve several areas, such as Calgary and Airdrie, or North Aurora and Naperville, make sure the link points to the right profile for that service location. Your Google Business Profile setup should be accurate before you push traffic to it.

Build a simple three message routine

Do not rely on memory. Use a routine that runs the same way every time. Message one goes out within an hour or two of finishing the job: a short thank you with the review link. Message two goes out two or three days later to anyone who has not responded, with a friendly reminder and no pressure. Stop there. A third nudge starts to feel pushy. Keep the wording personal: use the customer's first name, mention the type of clean, and say how much a review helps a small local business. A message like, thanks for choosing us, if you were happy with the clean, would you mind sharing a quick review, works well. You can send these by text, email or a booking tool. Pick the best customers to ask first

Not every customer is equally likely to leave a good, useful review. Start with the ones who have clearly been happy: recurring clients, people who thanked the crew, customers who rebooked, and commercial contacts who praise reliability. Ask them in person or by a quick call before you send the link, since a personal ask beats a cold text. After a move out clean, a deep clean or a one time job, the customer is often most delighted at the end of the day, so time your message then. Do not filter out unhappy customers in a way that blocks them from reviewing, because that can break Google's rules. Instead, fix problems quickly by phone first. Many unhappy customers become happy ones, and some then leave a review about how well you handled it.

Give customers an easy way to say what to write

Many people want to help but freeze at a blank box. Help them without writing the review for them. In your message, suggest a few things they might mention: the type of service, the neighborhood, how the crew treated their home, whether the team was on time, and what stood out. For example, a customer in Calgary NW might mention the deep clean of their kitchen. A commercial client might mention the nightly service in their office. Detailed reviews help future customers and also give search engines clear context about your services and areas. Never script or copy the same review for different people, and never post reviews on behalf of customers. Google can remove reviews that look fake or coordinated, so keep everything genuine.

Handle negative and unfair reviews calmly

Every business gets a bad review eventually. The way you answer matters more than the star rating. Wait until you have calmed down, then reply in a polite, factual tone. Thank them for the feedback, apologize that the experience fell short, and offer to fix the problem offline with a phone number or email. Avoid sharing private details about the job or the customer. Do not argue, blame or make excuses. If a review is clearly fake, from a competitor or breaks Google's policies, you can flag it for removal, but do not count on it working. Most readers judge you by how you respond. A calm reply to a bad review often builds more trust than a page of perfect scores, and it shows that real people run the company.

Reply to good reviews in a useful way

A reply is a tiny piece of content on your profile. Do more than say thanks. Use the reviewer's name, mention the service, such as the move out clean or weekly office cleaning, and add a short human line. You can naturally include the area you served, like the SW part of Calgary or Aurora, but do not stuff keywords. Keep each reply different. Responding to every review within a few days shows that your business is active. It also encourages other customers to leave their own. Set aside a short time each week to reply, and assign it to one person so it does not slip. Use reviews across your website and marketing

Collect reviews once and use them in many places. Add a few strong quotes to your home page, service pages and quote form so visitors see proof right when they decide. Match the review to the page: a deep cleaning review on the deep cleaning page, a commercial one on the janitorial page. Include the reviewer's first name and area if they agree. Add review schema only if it follows search engine guidelines and reflects real reviews. Share a review or two on social media each month, and in your proposals for commercial clients. Avoid the review mistakes that get you in trouble

A few common mistakes can hurt you. Do not offer discounts, gifts or free cleans in exchange for reviews. Do not review your own company or have staff and family do it. Do not buy reviews or use services that promise a fast jump in the number. Do not set up a tablet in your office and have many customers review from the same device, since that can look suspicious. Do not ask for reviews only from customers you know will say yes in a pattern that looks staged. Follow the current rules from Google, because they change. In Canada and the US, there are also advertising rules about fake or misleading endorsements. Slow and genuine always beats fast and risky, and a single removal or suspension can set you back for months.

Measure your review progress

Track a few numbers each month: how many new reviews you received, your average rating, how many you replied to, and how many came from each service type or area. Compare against the number of jobs completed to see your review rate. If you finish many jobs but get few reviews, the problem is usually the ask, not the service. Test one change at a time, such as moving the message earlier or adding a QR code on receipts. Keep an eye on competitors in your market too, in Calgary, Edmonton or the Fox Valley, to see what a normal pace looks like. Do not chase a magic number. Aim for steady, recent reviews. Frequently Asked Questions

How many reviews do I need to rank?

There is no fixed number. Consistent, recent and genuine reviews matter most.

Can I ask customers for reviews by text?

Yes, with a direct link, as long as you follow Google's policies and do not incentivize reviews.

What if I get a bad review?

Reply politely, offer to fix the problem and move on. Do not argue publicly.

How often should I ask for Google reviews?

Ask after every completed job, as part of your standard routine. Spacing matters less than consistency. A steady trickle of new reviews each week looks more natural and works better than a big burst once or twice a year.

Does replying to reviews help my Google ranking?

Google has not promised that replies are a ranking factor, but they help in other ways. Replies show customers you care, encourage more reviews and give you a chance to mention your services naturally. Treat them as part of good customer care.

Can I ask customers to mention keywords in their review?

You can suggest topics, such as the service they received or the area, but you should not script reviews or tell people exactly what to write. Genuine, specific reviews in a customer's own words are safer and more convincing.

What should I do if a former employee or competitor leaves a fake review?

Flag the review through your Google Business Profile and explain why it breaks the policy. Reply calmly in public in case it stays up. Keep records of any evidence. Removal is not guaranteed, so keep collecting genuine reviews to balance it.
