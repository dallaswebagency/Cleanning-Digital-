12 Pages and Features Every Cleaning Company Website Needs in Canada

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · October 25, 2026 · 7 min read

A cleaning company website does not need hundreds of pages, but it does need the right ones. Here is what we build for cleaning companies in Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver, Toronto and beyond.

A clear home page

Say what you clean, where you work and how to get a quote within seconds. Use a strong headline and one primary button.

One page per core service

House cleaning, deep cleaning, move-out cleaning, office cleaning, carpet cleaning, window cleaning and so on. This is how you rank for specific searches. See how we structure them in our cleaning company website design service.

City and area pages

Pages for Calgary, Airdrie, Cochrane or your own service areas help local rankings when each page has real content.

Quote and contact page

Short form, phone number, email and service area. Test it often. Our online booking and quote systems make this easier.

About and team page

People let cleaners into their homes, so show who you are. Real photos build more trust than stock images.

Reviews and testimonials

Show real reviews and link to your Google profile. Our reviews page is an example of presenting proof clearly.

Pricing guidance

Even a range or an explanation of what affects price reduces wasted calls. Read our website cost guide for how we explain pricing.

FAQ page and schema

Answer insurance, supplies, cancellations and scheduling. Add FAQ schema so search engines understand the answers.

Portfolio or results

Show before and after photos or website designs, like those in our work gallery.

Fast mobile experience

Most visitors use phones. Compress images and keep pages light.

Blog or resources

Helpful posts on cleaning topics and your services bring in extra traffic and build authority.

Plan the site map before you design anything

The best cleaning websites start with a simple map on paper. List your main services, your service areas, the questions customers ask and the proof you can show. Then decide which pages are required at launch and which can wait. A typical Canadian cleaning company might start with a home page, four to six service pages, a few area pages, about, reviews, FAQ, quote and contact. Group pages in the menu in a way a stranger would understand, such as Residential, Commercial and About. Keep the main menu short, with a clear quote button. This planning step saves money later because designers and developers know exactly what to build. Our cleaning company website design process begins here, and our pricing page explains how scope affects cost.

Write your home page for the first ten seconds

A visitor in Calgary, Edmonton, Toronto or Vancouver decides very quickly whether you are right for them. The top of your home page should say what you do, where you do it and how to get a quote. Add a phone number that is tappable on mobile. Below that, show a few trust signals: review stars from a real source, years in business if you have them, insurance and background-checked staff if true, and a short list of services. Then give a simple three step explanation of how working with you goes. Avoid long paragraphs about your company history at the top. Visitors can read that later on the about page. Keep one main action on the screen at a time so people do not freeze.

Make service pages that answer the real questions

A strong service page explains what is included, what is not, how long it usually takes, how you price it and what the customer needs to do beforehand. Add a short section on who the service suits, such as busy families, landlords or office managers. Include photos from real jobs and a few reviews related to that service. End with a clear quote button. Use the words customers use, like deep cleaning, move-out cleaning or janitorial service, in headings and in the first paragraph. Avoid copying the same text across pages. If you do commercial work, build separate pages for offices, medical clinics, retail and industrial sites, which matches how procurement people search. Create area pages that are genuinely useful

Area pages can help you appear in local searches, but only when they are real. A page for Calgary can mention the quadrants you cover, the types of homes you often clean and how scheduling works. A page for Airdrie, Cochrane or Okotoks can mention travel arrangements and which services are popular there. Add local reviews if you have them. Do not clone the same page for twenty towns, which Google treats as thin content. Link area pages to the relevant service pages and your profile. For US work, such as North Aurora, Illinois, the same approach applies with Fox Valley towns. Our local SEO for cleaning companies service shows how these pages fit into a longer plan. Start with your top three areas and add more as you grow.

Design the quote page as a conversion tool

Your quote page is where interest turns into revenue, so test it often. Ask only for what you need: name, phone, email, postal code, service type and a short note. For residential cleaning, you might add home size and number of bathrooms. For commercial, add building type and preferred schedule. Explain what happens next and how fast you reply. Add a phone option for people who dislike forms. Make the button text specific, such as Get my free quote. After submit, show a thank you page and send an email confirmation. Test from your phone every month and confirm that notifications reach the right inbox. A system like our online booking and quote tools can reduce back and forth. Show proof: reviews, photos, certifications and guarantees

Cleaning is a trust business. Collect proof and place it where decisions happen, not only on a testimonial page. Use real Google reviews near quote buttons, before and after photos on service pages and badges for insurance, WCB coverage or association memberships where they are accurate. If you offer a satisfaction guarantee, describe it plainly, including how to report a problem. Introduce your team with real photos, first names and a short line each. Do not use AI generated pictures of owners or staff, because customers notice and trust falls. If you lack photos, plan a simple photo day. Cover the legal and technical essentials for Canada

Canadian sites need a privacy policy that explains what you collect through forms and analytics, and it should follow rules such as PIPEDA, plus Quebec's requirements if you serve that province. Add a short terms page or service agreement summary. Use cookie notices if you run tracking tools that require consent. Keep prices and taxes clear, noting GST or HST where it applies. Add accessibility basics: readable contrast, alt text for images, keyboard friendly menus and labelled form fields. Use HTTPS, back up the site, and keep software updated. On the technical side, add schema markup, a sitemap and a custom 404 page. This is not legal advice, so check with a professional for your situation. Launch checklist and what to do in the first 90 days

Before launch, test every form, phone link and button on both phone and desktop. Check titles, headings and image sizes. Set up analytics and call tracking, connect Search Console and submit the sitemap. Redirect old URLs so you do not lose rankings. Update your Google profile and other listings to match the new site. After launch, watch for errors, review leads weekly and collect feedback from customers. In the first ninety days, add a few helpful blog posts, publish new photos and keep gathering reviews. Compare calls and quotes with your previous months. If something is not working, change one thing at a time. For an extra pair of eyes, book a discovery call. Write copy that sounds Canadian and local

Small details tell visitors you are a real local business. Use Canadian spelling such as colour and neighbour, show prices and taxes in Canadian dollars with GST or HST noted, and list phone numbers with local area codes. Mention the neighbourhoods, quadrants or boroughs you serve, and reference seasons that matter, like spring melt, winter salt and wildfire smoke season. If you serve French speaking customers in Quebec or New Brunswick, consider offering key pages in French. Keep sentences short and avoid jargon like deep sanitization protocols unless you explain them. Read your copy aloud and ask a friend outside the business whether it is clear. Good local copy supports your rankings, which we explain in our Calgary client guide, and it makes the site feel like it was made for the person reading it.

Measure which pages actually earn quote requests

Once the site is live, look at the data. Set up goals for form submissions, phone taps and email clicks. Each month, see which pages bring visitors and which bring enquiries. A page with plenty of traffic but no quotes may need a clearer button, better proof or simpler copy. A page with few visitors but strong conversion may deserve more links and promotion. Use heatmaps or session recordings sparingly to spot confusion, and always respect privacy rules. Ask new customers how they found you and what nearly stopped them from calling. Make one change at a time so you know what worked. This feedback loop is where websites become real marketing assets rather than online brochures. Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages should a cleaning company website have?

Most do well with eight to fifteen pages at launch, then add more as they grow.

Do I need a blog?

It is not required, but helpful posts can bring in extra traffic and answer customer questions.

Can you build a site for a cleaning company outside Calgary?

Yes. We work with cleaning companies across Canada and in US cities such as North Aurora, Illinois.

Do I need separate pages for residential and commercial cleaning?

Yes, in most cases. The customers, questions and buying process are different. Separate pages let you speak to each group clearly and target different searches without confusing either audience.

Should my cleaning website show prices?

It depends on your services. Many owners show starting points or explain what affects price, such as home size, condition and frequency. This filters out poor fits and builds trust without locking you into a single number.

How often should I update my cleaning website?

Review it at least quarterly. Update photos, reviews, service details and seasonal offers, and run software updates and backups regularly. Small, steady changes keep the site accurate and help search engines see it as active.

Can a cleaning website rank in cities where I do not have an office?

It can, if you genuinely serve that city and have useful content for it. Expect it to be harder than ranking near your base. Reviews, local pages and a strong profile all help.
